# When NOT to Use Sampling - Question Analysis

## The Question
Which of the following is **not** a good scenario to use sampling?

## Answer: Option 3 - Press 3
**"You've finished collecting data on a small study of the wing measurements for 10 butterflies."**

## Explanation

When you already have a very small dataset (only 10 butterflies), sampling is unnecessary and counterproductive. Here's why:

### Why Option 3 is NOT a good scenario for sampling:
- **Dataset is already tiny**: With only 10 data points, any sample would be even smaller and likely not representative
- **Complete analysis is feasible**: 10 measurements can easily be analyzed in full without computational burden
- **Sampling would reduce statistical power**: Taking a subset of 10 would give you insufficient data for meaningful analysis
- **Risk of losing important information**: In such a small dataset, every data point matters

### Why the other options ARE good scenarios for sampling:

**Option 1 (1 terabyte of error logs)**: 
- Massive dataset requiring significant computational resources
- Sampling can provide insights without processing the entire dataset

**Option 2 (Travel habits of all Pakistani adults)**:
- Population too large to survey completely
- Well-designed sampling can provide representative insights

**Option 4 (Big data customer turnover prediction)**:
- Large dataset where sampling can speed up model development
- Can use sampling for initial exploration and validation

## Key Principle
Sampling is most valuable when datasets are large and complete analysis is computationally expensive or practically impossible. When you already have a small, manageable dataset, use all available data for maximum statistical power and accuracy.
